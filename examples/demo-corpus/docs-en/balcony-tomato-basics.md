The most common reason balcony tomatoes fail is not the wrong variety — it is too little sun and too small a pot. Tomatoes are sun-lovers, needing at least six hours of direct light a day before they will flower and set fruit, and no amount of feeding will make up for a shaded balcony.

## Pots and growing medium

For a home balcony, choose a small-fruited or cherry variety; the root system is more modest and suits a ten to fifteen litre pot. Use a peat-based mix with perlite for drainage. Waterlogging at the bottom of the pot, and the root rot that follows, is what kills more balcony tomatoes than anything else.

## Watering rhythm

Tomatoes dislike swinging between bone dry and soaking. Let the surface of the soil dry, then water thoroughly. During fruiting this matters most: erratic watering is what splits the fruit. Watering early in the morning beats watering at dusk, as it leaves less overnight humidity for disease to take hold in.

## Staking and side shoots

Stake the plant once it reaches about thirty centimetres, before the weight of fruit can snap a stem. Tomatoes throw out side shoots from every leaf axil continuously; unless you deliberately want a bushier plant carrying more but smaller fruit, pinch them out regularly so the plant's energy goes into the main stem and the fruit it is already carrying.
